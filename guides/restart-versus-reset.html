Router restart versus factory reset: know the difference

A restart is a diagnostic step. A factory reset can erase the very settings you need to get back online.

SignalNook EditorialReviewed 11 Nov 2026
THE SHORT ANSWER

A restart is a diagnostic step. A factory reset can erase the very settings you need to get back online. Begin with a repeatable comparison and record one change at a time, rather than making several router changes together.

Restart: a temporary power cycle

Restarting a router lets it reload software and renegotiate connections. It usually preserves the configured network name, password and port settings, though behavior varies by manufacturer. A normal reboot is often reasonable during a general outage after checking for provider notices and active updates. If you use both a modem and router, follow the ISP or manufacturer’s recommended restart order.

Reset: a return to defaults

A factory reset may remove Wi-Fi names, passwords, ISP authentication, static address reservations, VPN settings, parental controls and guest networks. It can leave the home with no usable connection until the router is configured again. On some devices, a short press and a long press of a button have different meanings. Never guess which one you are doing.

Decide from evidence

If two devices fail to reach the internet and the ISP reports an outage, a factory reset is unlikely to help. If you are trying to recover access to a router whose administrator password was lost, the manufacturer may eventually recommend a reset—but only after you understand the recovery steps and can restore required credentials.

Document before acting

Capture the model, Wi-Fi SSID, ISP requirements and any unusual settings you intentionally configured. Export a configuration backup if the vendor supports it and store it securely. A photo of the router back may identify the model, but avoid posting serial numbers or network passwords publicly.

After the change

A successful restart should be followed by checks on several devices and any special services such as guest Wi-Fi or smart-home hubs. If the same issue returns quickly, look for logs, firmware health and repeated provider problems. A restart that temporarily hides an intermittent fault is not a lasting diagnosis.

A worked example

Example: the internet drops every evening, and restarting restores access for an hour. Repeating a reboot daily masks the pattern; a diary of times and router WAN status is more helpful for identifying the underlying fault.

How to interpret three common observations

The same visible symptom can have more than one cause. Use the comparisons below as test priorities, not as an automatic diagnosis of your particular equipment.

Service is down and ISP confirms outage

Do not factory-reset gateway credentials; wait for supported service restoration and record outage duration.

Router responds slowly but retains service

A planned restart may be a reasonable reversible test if updates are not running; verify behavior afterward.

Admin credentials are lost

A factory reset might eventually be required, but only after obtaining the provider or vendor recovery instructions.

Field checklist

  • Check for outages first
  • Read vendor reset/reboot instructions
  • Save configuration where supported
  • Choose restart for reversible testing
  • Reset only with a recovery plan
Don't make this mistake

Do not hold an unknown pinhole button while the router is updating or while essential connectivity is needed.
