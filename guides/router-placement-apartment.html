Coverage / GUIDE NO. 06

Find a better router position in a small apartment

A good placement test costs nothing and often teaches more than a product comparison page.

SignalNook EditorialReviewed 11 Nov 2026
THE SHORT ANSWER

A good placement test costs nothing and often teaches more than a product comparison page. Begin with a repeatable comparison and record one change at a time, rather than making several router changes together.

Map where Wi-Fi is actually needed

Sketch your apartment: home office, TV area, bedroom, kitchen and the location where the broadband line enters. Mark the rooms with weak performance instead of relying on signal bars alone. A centrally located router is a good starting hypothesis, but a cable entry at an outer wall may limit realistic placements. The best location balances coverage, power access, safe cable runs and the rooms where reliable service matters most.

Favor open air over concealment

Routers tucked behind televisions, packed in metal cabinets or placed at floor level may have a harder path to devices. If practical, move one to an open, elevated shelf away from dense obstacles and large electronics. Do not block ventilation or compromise safe power placement. When the router has external antennas, use the manufacturer’s orientation guidance rather than copying an internet photo.

Run a paired test

Test the original location during a quiet period: near router, office, bedroom. Move it to a permitted alternative and retest from the same positions with the same device. Record connectivity and practical tasks such as a call or video stream, not only headline speed. If moving helps one area but harms another, consider whether a wired access point provides a more balanced layout.

Read your walls and floor plan

A thin partition may behave differently from reinforced concrete, tile, plumbing or foil-backed insulation. A corridor can offer a cleaner path than a direct line through multiple walls. Moving a router only one shelf or one room may alter the number of obstacles substantially. There is no universal “place it in the center” coordinate for every home.

Use our comparison tool honestly

The Placement Compare worksheet scores candidate positions based on choices you enter; it does not read radio strength, recognize your walls or simulate a real Wi-Fi signal. Use its output to plan the next physical test and then keep the location that performs better in your home.

A worked example

Example: an apartment router sits inside a media cabinet beside the entrance. Moving it to an open shelf near the corridor improves office coverage without buying another device.

How to interpret three common observations

The same visible symptom can have more than one cause. Use the comparisons below as test priorities, not as an automatic diagnosis of your particular equipment.

Move from metal cabinet to open shelf

The change reduces nearby obstructions. Retest the office and bedroom; a stronger signal next to the router alone is not sufficient evidence.

New position helps bedroom but harms desk

Choose based on the rooms that matter, or assess a second access point with safe wired backhaul rather than chasing one perfect position.

Both locations deliver the same weak wired baseline

Wireless placement is not the main limiting factor. Check the internet service and capable Ethernet hardware first.

Field checklist

  • Map important rooms
  • Photograph or note the current position
  • Select one safe alternative position
  • Retest on the same device and locations
  • Keep whichever placement improves real tasks
Don't make this mistake

Never route power cables unsafely, block ventilation, or relocate ISP hardware without following its instructions.
